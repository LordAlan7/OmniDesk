# Team’s working agreements

## Rules for attendance

It’s expected for every team member to attend every Friday from 9:00 AM to 10:00 AM meeting with the professor.

As a team we are going to have weekly meetings on Monday from 8:30 AM to 9:00 AM to review the feedback given by the professor and to plan the week's work.

Friday meetings have no excuses.

Monday meetings can be excused but that team member has to ask other team members for the week’s work plan.

## Participation

Every team member has the ability to propose an implementation for the project, these propositions have to happen on mondays but can happen every day of the week.

Every new proposition by any team member has to have feedback from other team members, we believe that good ideas are created by cooperation.

Every commit made to parts of the project that are critical for the operation has to be reviewed by the QA member.

## Deadlines

Changes to critical parts of the project can’t be made on Friday AM.

Important tasks are expected to be made on time, team members are expected to follow the priority criteria (Important - Urgent - Impact) prioritizing the task with a “High” label of priority.

If a critical task is not done by Thursday there is a special exception for all the team members to finish that task with special review by the QA member.

## Task assignment

Tasks will be assigned during the Monday team meeting based on the project's priorities, individual roles, skills, and current workload.

Each task must have a clearly defined responsible team member, priority level, and expected deadline.

Tasks should be assigned according to the team's priority criteria: Important - Urgent - Impact.

Team members may propose tasks or volunteer for tasks when they have the necessary skills.

Critical tasks must be reviewed by the QA member before being considered completed.

If a task requires collaboration between multiple members, one member will be designated as the primary responsible person.

## Decision-making

Technical decisions should be discussed by the members involved in the affected area of the project.

The team will prioritize decisions based on project requirements, technical feasibility, security, maintainability, and available time.

The member responsible for a specific area will coordinate the decision, while other affected members may provide feedback.

Major decisions that affect the scope, deadlines, security, architecture, or overall project direction must involve all four team members.

Decisions must be documented in the project documentation and communicated to the entire team before implementation.

When an agreement cannot be reached, the team will discuss the available alternatives and select the option that provides the greatest benefit to the project.

## Conflict resolution

Team members should first attempt to resolve conflicts through respectful and direct communication.

Technical disagreements must be discussed using project requirements, evidence, testing results, and technical feasibility rather than personal opinions.

If the conflict affects the project and cannot be resolved by the members involved, the Project Manager will coordinate a team discussion.

For conflicts involving technical quality or testing, the QA member may provide an independent evaluation.

If the team cannot reach an agreement, the decision will be documented along with the reasons for selecting the final solution.

Personal conflicts must not affect project responsibilities, communication, or deadlines.

## Missed commitments & changes of responsibilities

If a team member realizes that they will not be able to complete an assigned task on time, they must notify the team as soon as possible.

The team will evaluate the task's priority, impact, and remaining time before deciding how to proceed.

When necessary, a task may be reassigned to another team member based on skills, workload, and availability.

The original responsible member should provide the necessary information, progress, files, or code to the new responsible member to avoid delays.

Changes in responsibilities must be communicated to the entire team and documented in the project management records.

If a critical task is missed by Thursday, the team may work together to complete it, following the special QA review rule already established.

Repeated missed commitments will be discussed during the team meeting to identify the cause and adjust workload or responsibilities.

| **Goal Statement** | Develop a centralized IT Service and Knowledge Management Platform that enables employees to submit and track support requests, enables support personnel to manage incidents through a structured workflow, centralizes technical knowledge, and uses an AI support assistant to provide relevant troubleshooting guidance based on authorized organizational information. | | |
| **Description and Background** | A medium-sized organization currently provides technical support through email, messaging applications, telephone calls, and informal communication with IT personnel. As the number of employees and systems increases, support personnel repeatedly solve the same problems, while valuable technical knowledge remains distributed among individuals and old documents. The organization therefore wants a centralized IT Service and Knowledge Management Platform. Employees should be able to create support requests, describe problems, attach evidence, and monitor their requests. Support personnel should be able to classify, prioritize, assign, investigate, resolve, and close incidents. An important component is an AI support assistant capable of analyzing a user's problem and searching the organization's technical knowledge base to suggest possible solutions. The team must investigate and select an appropriate AI approach, such as a RAG architecture over technical documentation, an internally deployed LLM, or a hybrid architecture in which an LLM interacts with the organization's knowledge base and support-ticket history. | | |
| **Objectives** | Develop a centralized platform for managing IT support requests.  Implement a structured workflow for incident registration, classification, prioritization, assignment, resolution, and closure.  Centralize technical documentation and organizational knowledge.  Reduce repetitive work performed by support personnel.  Provide users with visibility into the status of their support requests.  Develop an AI assistant capable of interpreting support requests and providing relevant troubleshooting guidance.  Implement an AI architecture capable of retrieving information from authorized organizational documentation.  Evaluate the quality, accuracy, relevance, and limitations of AI-generated recommendations.  Provide mechanisms for transferring AI-assisted cases to human support personnel when necessary.  Generate metrics that allow the organization to evaluate support performance.  Apply formal project-management practices throughout the development process. | | |
| **Scope** | The project includes employee support-request creation and tracking; incident registration, classification, prioritization, assignment, investigation, resolution, and closure; centralized technical documentation and organizational knowledge; an AI support assistant that interprets support requests and retrieves information from authorized organizational documentation; escalation or transfer of AI-assisted cases to human support personnel; support-performance metrics; and evaluation of candidate AI architectures, including RAG, local LLM, external API, or hybrid approaches. The initial knowledge base is limited to a defined subset of technical problems within the academic project timeframe. | | |
| **Deliverables** | Centralized IT support-request management platform.  Employee interface for creating requests, attaching evidence, and tracking status.  Support-personnel workflow for classifying, prioritizing, assigning, investigating, resolving, and closing incidents.  Centralized technical knowledge base.  AI support assistant connected to authorized organizational knowledge.  Human-support escalation/transfer mechanism for cases that require human intervention.  Support-performance metrics and reporting capabilities.  Evaluation of the selected AI approach, including quality, accuracy, relevance, and limitations of its recommendations.  Project-management documentation and development evidence. | | |
