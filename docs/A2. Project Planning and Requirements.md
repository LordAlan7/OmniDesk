## **Problem and business-context analysis** 

### **Refined problem statement** 

An unorganized IT team can be less effective than a team that solves problems slowly. When users encounter technical issues, there may be no centralized system to report, track, prioritize, and assign those problems to the appropriate IT personnel. This can lead to duplicated efforts, unclear responsibilities, delayed responses to high-priority issues, and inefficient use of IT resources. 

Therefore, there is a need for a centralized platform that allows users to report problems and enables IT personnel to clearly visualize, prioritize, assign, and track them throughout the resolution process. And with the implementation of AI problems that have been limiting IT teams and their platforms can be solved and even refined. 

### **Current situation/process (AS-IS)** 

Currently, the company does not have a centralized IT ticketing system. Technical problems, referred to as “store issues,” are mainly reported through Outlook email or WhatsApp, with approximately 15 issues received per week. The service advisor on duty initially receives the problem and is responsible for monitoring and following up on it. 

The reported information usually includes the problem description, problem type, store location, motorcycle model, and other relevant information. The company currently handles three types of issues: assembled, activated, and store issues. However, there is no centralized platform where these problems can be registered, prioritized, assigned, or tracked. 

The manager in charge of the shift is responsible for defining the priority of an issue, while the service advisor is responsible for assigning and following up on it. In addition, the company works with multiple manuals and scattered sources of information, which makes it difficult for employees to find specific information and receive consistent training.

### **Pain points, limitations and opportunities** 

_Pain Points and limitations_ 

- Problems are reported through different channels, mainly WhatsApp and email. 

- Important information is distributed across emails, messages, manuals, and other sources, making it difficult to find. 

- There is no centralized way to visualize pending issues, their priorities, deadlines, or current status. 

- Service advisors have to monitor and follow up on problems without a dedicated system. 

- Employees need to consult different manuals and sources to find information related to their work. 

- There is no centralized library of previous solutions that employees can consult when the same or a similar problem occurs. 

- Notifications and problem reports arrive through external channels, making it harder to maintain a complete record of the issues. 

##### _Opportunities_ 

- Create a centralized ticketing platform to register, prioritize, assign, and track issues. 

- Implement a dashboard to visualize pending tasks, priorities, deadlines, and daily activities. 

- Create a solution and knowledge library containing manuals and previous solutions. 

- Implement an AI training assistant that can answer questions and provide relevant information from the company's manuals. 

- Use AI to provide recommendations for solving recurring problems. 

- Explore automated ticket creation from incoming reports. 

- Explore AI-assisted quotations or estimates, considering that labor costs may vary. 

### **Desired situation (TO-BE)** 

The desired situation is to have a centralized IT service and knowledge management platform where employees can report and track problems without relying on WhatsApp or email as the primary method of communication. 

Users should be able to create tickets containing the relevant information, such as the problem description, motorcycle type, problem type, creation date, priority, deadline, and store location. Managers should be able to define priorities, while service advisors should be able to assign and monitor tickets from a centralized interface. 

##### **Software Projects Management** 

The platform should also provide a dashboard where employees can visualize pending issues, priorities, deadlines, and daily tasks. This would give the company better visibility over its current workload and help organize the follow-up process. 

In addition, the platform should centralize manuals, previous solutions, and other relevant information into a knowledge base. An AI assistant could use this information to help employees find relevant documentation, recommend possible solutions, and support employee training. 

The system could also incorporate automation for ticket creation, reports, and recurring problem identification, as well as explore AI-assisted quotations based on available information about labor costs. This would reduce the dependence on scattered communication channels, improve access to information, and make the process of handling problems more organized and efficient.

For this platform, the functional requirements would mainly focus on managing support requests, allowing users to create and track incidents, assigning and prioritizing requests, managing the knowledge base, recording solutions, generating reports, and providing an AI assistant that can analyze problems and suggest solutions based on authorized technical information. ### **What does the project will and will not include** 

As a first glance on the interview with the Italika stakeholder we see it as a good opportunity to implement not only a tickets system but also a platform where AI can help to solve problems faster, document every solution and also to create an ecosystem where the service advisor/IT manager can assign tickets faster to the technicians on the shift. The only implementation that we are currently considering is the access of the clients of our stakeholders to the system, whether they can access and post the tickets personally or if the advisor/manager inputs the tickets to the system after a meeting with the client. 

After the first big description of the project this is the list of the major implementations and the major things that the project will and will not include 

##### _Will include_ 

- A tickets platform where clients issues can be distributed among the technicians. 

- A dashboard for the stakeholder to see and analyze the metrics of the store. 

- ● A system where the client is either able to enter the platform to follow their own tickets or to receive a message to update the situation of the ticket. 

- The ability for the stakeholder to manage their technicians and see their metrics. 

- A library of reports where previous tickets and their solutions are stored for the technicians to find solutions to similar problems faster. 

- A library managed by AI and by the stakeholder where manuals can be stored to find a solution to the client’s tickets faster. 

##### _Will not include_ 

- AI giving the exact solution to a problem, full automation can’t be made due to the necessity of a technician to review the solution and make the right decision. 

- A point of sale (POS) platform that currently we have in mind is directed to the solution and administration of tickets for technicians. 

- As stated on “will include” we are currently exploring the option of doing a partial CRM where the user can enter the platform to view the current situation of their tickets. But currently the option is not on our plans.

### **System boundaries** 

The current system boundaries are the limitations with the AI, a local LLM would be perfect due to the options that it gives without amping up the cost of use, our system is limited right now in that sense because if we want to fully implement AI we have to use free tiers and AIs that give more freedom without costing anything or much. 

Also, when you are considering an AI to solve technical problems you can not give the AI full freedom to give the solution to the technician to follow by the stem, technicians have to be aware that the solutions might or might not be correct and to provide their human expertise and criteria to solve the tickets. 

Another big boundary is the user participation on the system, we have to give the option for the user to either use the platform to follow the status of their ticket or to give updates either by mail or by whatsapp, some people don’t care about a system where updates are given, but some other see it as a necessity, we are currently exploring this option so it can change from a boundary to a requirement.

A dashboard where the stakeholder can see the current situation of the store through metrics like number of tickets, resolved tickets, pending tickets, technician performance and other relevant information. 

- Library of solutions 

A library where previous tickets, reports and their solutions can be stored. Technicians will be able to search this information when they have a similar problem and use previous solutions as a reference. 

- AI assistance module 

A module where AI can use the information from the knowledge base, manuals and previous tickets to help the technician investigate a problem and find possible solutions faster. The AI will work as an assistant and not as the final decision maker. 

##### **Software Projects Management** 

Also AI will help technicians to fill the reports to ensure they are usable for future references. 

- Manuals and documentation module 

A library managed by the stakeholder where technical manuals and other relevant documentation can be stored. This information can be used by the technicians and the AI when investigating a ticket. 

- Client ticket follow-up module 

A module that will allow us to explore the possibility of giving clients access to their tickets. Depending on the final decision, clients could enter the platform to see the status of their tickets or receive updates through email or WhatsApp. 

### **Initial assumptions and constraints** 

The first assumption is that the stakeholder will have an active role in the system. The service advisor/IT manager will be responsible for managing the technicians, assigning tickets and reviewing the information generated by the system. The system will not replace the decisions made by the stakeholder or the technicians. 

Another assumption is that the knowledge base will grow as the system is used. Previous tickets and their solutions will be stored so they can be used as references for future problems. The manuals and technical documentation will also depend on the information provided by the stakeholder. 

One of the main constraints is the cost of the AI implementation. A local LLM would be a good option because it could give us more freedom without increasing the cost of using the system. However, depending on the resources available for the project, we may have to use free tiers or AI services with limited usage. 

Another constraint is the reliability of the AI-generated solutions. The AI will not be able to give an exact solution that the technician must follow. Its responses will have to be considered as recommendations or possible solutions, and the technician will have to review the information and use their own knowledge and criteria before taking action. 

The participation of the clients is another current constraint. We still have to decide if clients will directly enter the platform to create and follow their tickets or if the service advisor/IT manager will create the tickets after talking with the client. The same applies to the way clients will receive updates, either through the platform, email or WhatsApp. 

Finally, the project is currently focused on IT service and ticket administration. Because of this, features outside this main purpose, such as a complete CRM or a POS system, are not considered part of the current scope unless the stakeholder identifies them as necessary during the development.

The development of the project will have different dependencies between its modules and activities. **The dashboard and metrics will depend on the tickets module** because the information shown in the dashboard will come from the tickets and their current status, resolution time, assigned technician and other information generated during the process. 

**The knowledge base will depend on the tickets and reports modules** because previous tickets and their solutions will be used as information for technicians to find solutions to similar problems. 

##### **Software Projects Management** 

**The AI module will depend on the knowledge base and documentation modules** . The AI will need access to manuals, previous tickets and their solutions to provide useful recommendations when investigating a problem. 

**The client follow-up module will depend on the final decision made with the stakeholder about how clients will interact with the system** . If clients are allowed to access the platform directly, the system will need the corresponding authentication and permissions. If the stakeholder decides to manage the tickets for the clients, this module could instead focus on sending updates through email or WhatsApp. 

**Testing will also be dependent on the development of each module.** Each module will need to have a functional version before its corresponding tests can be executed, while integration testing will depend on having the main modules connected.
