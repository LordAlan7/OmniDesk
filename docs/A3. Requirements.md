## Purpose

The project, as stated in the pass document, aims to solve a big problem among platforms and departments where solutions are needed. Sometimes, specially in small IT departments, tickets and problems are not followed along, the problems get lost due to a poor or a lack of administration and prioritization. Also, less experienced technicians might find themselves lacking enough experience and they could feel lost without proper guidance. Traceability is also a powerful tool when dealing with this type of departments/solutions.

Our software solution aims to solve this problem, integrating all those solutions into a platform that improves traceability, tasks monitoring, time to solve a task, etc. The solutions aims not only to improve the efficiency of technicians but also speed and to solve accountability problems.

## Project background

We found that the most recurrent problems in IT departments and workshops were lack of accountability, lack of traceability, bad prioritization and lack of organization with past tickets.

All the problems are aimed to be solved using the platform. And the main integration that aims to solve a lot of problems is the integration of AI that uses past tickets to give solutions to current problems.

## Problem addressed

The project solves the problems related to the administration and follow-up of tickets in IT departments and workshops. Problems such as tickets getting lost, bad prioritization, lack of traceability and lack of accountability can make it difficult to solve problems correctly and on time.The platform provides a centralized place where tickets can be created, assigned, prioritized and followed until they are solved. It also keeps the information from past tickets, which can be used by technicians when they find similar problems.

The AI integration also helps technicians by using past tickets and their solutions to provide possible solutions for current problems. This can be especially useful for less experienced technicians who may need guidance when solving a problem.

## Intended audience

The main users of the platform are IT departments and their technicians. The platform is intended for technicians who need to receive, manage, prioritize and solve tickets from other employees or users.

It is also intended for IT managers or people responsible for the department, who need to monitor the tickets, assign tasks, check their progress and have a better view of the department's workload.

The AI functionality is mainly intended to help technicians when they need guidance to solve a problem, especially when they have less experience with a specific type of issue.

# Product overview

## Product perspective

Omnidesk is a centralized IT service and knowledge management platform designed to improve the way technical support requests are registered, managed, resolved, and documented. The system replaces fragmented support processes based on email, messaging apps, telephone calls and informal communication with a structured digital workflow.

The platform will integrate several related components: a ticket management module, user and role management, a centralized technical knowledge base, dashboards and reporting, and AI support assistant. These components will work together so that employees can report and track issues while IT support personnel can classify, prioritize, assign, investigate, resolve and close tickets.

The AI component will support users and IT personnel by interpreting support requests and retrieving relevant information from authorized organizational documentation. Depending on the results of the feasibility analysis, the AI may be implemented using a Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) architecture, a local Large Language Model (LLM), an external AI API, or a hybrid approach. AI recommendations will remain advisory and will require human review when necessary.

OmniDesk is therefore positioned as a support and knowledge-management platform rather than a fully autonomous IT solution. Human support personnel remain responsible for making final decisions, particularly for complex or critical incidents. The platform will also provide metrics and reports that allow managers to monitor support performance and identify recurring problems.

## Product objectives

The main objectives of OmniDesk are:

Develop a centralized platform for managing IT support requests.

Implement a structured workflow for ticket registration, classification, prioritization, assignment, investigation, resolution, and closure.

Centralize technical documentation and organizational knowledge.

Reduce repetitive work performed by support personnel by making previous solutions and technical documentation easier to access.

Provide employees with visibility into the status and progress of their support requests.

Provide an AI support assistant capable of interpreting support requests and suggesting relevant troubleshooting guidance.

Ensure that AI recommendations are based on authorized organizational information whenever organization-specific knowledge is required.

Provide mechanisms for transferring AI-assisted cases to human support personnel when AI assistance is insufficient or inappropriate.

Generate dashboards, metrics, and reports that allow support performance to be monitored.

Evaluate the quality, accuracy, relevance, and limitations of the selected AI approach.

Protect sensitive organizational information through appropriate user roles and permissions.

Provide a modular platform that can support future expansion of the knowledge base and system functionality.

## Actors

The main actors who interact directly with OmniDesk are:

### **Employee**

Employees use the platform to report technical problems and monitor their requests. They can provide problem descriptions, attach evidence, view ticket information, and follow the progress of their requests. Depending on the final implementation, employees may also receive updates through the platform or external communication channels.

### **Service Advisor**

The service advisor manages the operational distribution of support requests. This actor can review tickets and assign them to the personnel responsible for handling or following up on the issue. Service advisors can also monitor active work through the system dashboard.

### **Manager**

The manager has higher-level management responsibilities. This actor can define ticket priorities, monitor support performance, review dashboards and reports, and oversee the workload and progress of support personnel.

### **IT Support Personnel / Technician**

IT support personnel are responsible for investigating and resolving technical incidents. They can access assigned tickets, review the information and evidence provided, consult the knowledge base, use AI-generated recommendations as assistance, document solutions, and close resolved tickets.

### **System Administrator**

The system administrator manages system configuration, user accounts, roles, and permissions. This actor ensures that users can only access the information and functions authorized for their role.

### **AI Support Assistant**

The AI assistant is a system component rather than a human user. It analyzes support requests and retrieves relevant information from the authorized knowledge base to generate troubleshooting recommendations. Its recommendations must be clearly identified as AI-generated and must not be treated as guaranteed solutions.

### **Project Team / QA**

During development and testing, the project team interacts with the system to configure, develop, test, validate, and evaluate its functionality. The QA role is responsible for designing and executing tests and identifying defects.

## System boundaries

The system boundary defines what OmniDesk is responsible for and what remains outside the platform.

### **Inside the system boundary**

OmniDesk will include:

User authentication and role management.

Creation and management of support tickets.

Ticket classification and prioritization.

Assignment of tickets to support personnel.

Ticket investigation, resolution, updating, and closure.

Storage of ticket information such as problem description, motorcycle type or model, problem type, creation date, priority, deadline, and store location.

Employee access to ticket status and information.

Centralized technical documentation and knowledge management.

A library of recurring problems and documented solutions.

AI-assisted troubleshooting based on authorized organizational knowledge.

AI-assisted ticket creation where applicable.

References to manuals or knowledge articles used to support AI recommendations.

Dashboards for monitoring tickets, priorities, deadlines, and work progress.

Support-performance reports and metrics.

Human escalation or transfer when AI assistance is insufficient.

User permissions that restrict access to sensitive information.

These functions are consistent with the previously defined requirements for ticket management, dashboards, role management, knowledge reuse, reporting, and AI-assisted functionality.

### **Outside the system boundary**

The following are outside the current scope of OmniDesk:

A point-of-sale (POS) system.

Fully autonomous resolution of technical incidents by AI.

Automatic execution of potentially destructive administrative actions.

Allowing AI recommendations to replace the decisions of qualified support personnel.

A complete CRM system.

Management of business processes unrelated to IT support.

Unlimited technical knowledge coverage during the initial implementation.

Guaranteed solutions for every technical problem.

Replacement of human IT support personnel.

The initial knowledge base will cover only a limited and defined set of technical problems within the academic project timeframe. AI recommendations must remain advisory, and human personnel must make final decisions for complex or critical incidents.

### **External dependencies and limitations**

OmniDesk also depends on several external conditions. The organization must provide authorized technical documentation for the knowledge base, representative users and IT personnel must be available for feedback and testing, and the selected AI architecture must remain feasible within the project's available budget and timeframe.

The AI implementation is also constrained by the availability and cost of infrastructure or external AI APIs. The project will therefore evaluate alternatives such as a local LLM, external API, RAG, or a hybrid architecture before selecting the final solution.

## Current problem

The company currently lacks a centralized system for managing technical issues reported by stores. Issues are primarily communicated through Outlook email and WhatsApp, which makes it difficult to maintain a consistent record of incidents and their current status.

This situation can result in:

Issues being difficult to track from report to resolution.

Unclear assignment of responsibilities.

Limited visibility into the priority and status of each issue.

Duplicate communication or efforts when multiple people become involved.

Delays in identifying and addressing high-priority problems.

Difficulty obtaining historical information about previously reported issues.

Limited ability to measure IT/service performance using historical data.

Approximately 15 issues are reported per week, making the absence of a centralized tracking mechanism increasingly relevant as the volume of requests grows.

## Current information sources

The information related to technical issues is currently distributed across different communication channels rather than being stored in a centralized system.

The main sources are:

| **Information Source** | **Current Use** |
| --- | --- |
| Outlook email | Reporting and communicating technical problems |
| WhatsApp | Reporting problems and communicating updates |
| Service advisor | Initial reception, follow-up, and assignment of issues |
| Shift manager | Determines the priority of reported issues |
| Store personal | Provides information about the problem and affected equipment |
| Issue information provided by the user | Problem description, issue type, store location, motorcycle model, and other relevant information |

The company currently identifies three types of issues:

Assembled issues

Activated issues

Store issues

However, this information is not currently consolidated into a single structured database or ticketing system. As a result, information about an issue may remain distributed across conversations, emails, and the people responsible for handling it.

## Identified opportunities

The current process presents several opportunities for improvement through the implementation of a centralized IT service platform.

**1. Centralize issue management**

Create a single location where technical issues can be registered, categorized, prioritized, assigned, updated, and tracked throughout their entire lifecycle.

This would provide IT personnel with a consistent view of pending, active, and resolved issues.

**2. Improve prioritization and assignment**

Establish a structured workflow in which issues can be assigned to the appropriate personnel according to their type, priority, location, or other relevant criteria.

This can reduce ambiguity regarding who is responsible for each issue.

**3. Create a centralized knowledge base**

Information from previously resolved issues can be organized into a knowledge base containing:

Common problems.

Solutions.

Troubleshooting procedures.

Frequently asked questions.

Technical documentation.

Historical incidents.

This allows recurring problems to be resolved using previously documented knowledge instead of starting the investigation from zero.

**4. Use AI to support problem resolution**

An AI assistant can be integrated with the authorized knowledge base to help IT personnel identify possible solutions, retrieve relevant documentation, and provide troubleshooting guidance.

The AI would therefore function as a support tool for the IT team, rather than replacing the personnel responsible for making decisions.

**5. Generate historical and operational data**

A centralized system would also create structured historical data that could be analyzed to identify:

Most frequent problems.

Most affected locations.

Average resolution time.

Number of issues by category.

Recurring incidents.

Workload per service advisor or IT personnel.

High-priority issue frequency.

This information could eventually support data-driven improvements to the IT support process.

They can also use the knowledge base and AI assistant when they need additional information.

The system may also connect with external services such as email or messaging platforms to send notifications and updates. The AI assistant may use an external AI service or a local AI model, depending on the available resources. The AI will use authorized manuals, documents, and previous solutions to provide support to users.

# Use Cases

## UC-001 – Create and Register a Support Ticket

| **Field** | **Description** |
| --- | --- |
| **ID** | UC-001 |
| **Name** | Create and Register a Support Ticket |
| **Primary Actor** | Employee |
| **Supporting Actors** | Service Advisor, System |
| **Goal** | Register a technical problem so it can be classified, prioritized, assigned, and followed up. |
| **Precondition** | The employee is authenticated and has permission to create tickets. |
| **Trigger** | The employee identifies a technical problem and needs IT support. |
| **Main flow** | 1. Employee accesses the ticket creation function.  2. Employee enters the problem description and required information.  3. Employee selects or provides the relevant problem information.  4. Employee attaches evidence if necessary.  5. System validates the information.  6. System creates a unique ticket.  7. System records the creation date and ticket information.  8. Ticket becomes available to authorized support personnel. |
| **Alternate flows** | If the employee has additional evidence, files can be attached to the ticket. If AI-assisted ticket creation is enabled, the AI may suggest information based on the employee's description. |
| **Exception flows** | If required information is missing, the system does not create the ticket and requests the missing information. If the system cannot save the ticket, an error is displayed and the ticket is not considered registered. |
| **Postconditions** | A ticket is registered with a unique identifier and can be reviewed by authorized support personnel. |
| **Related requirements** | FR-003, FR-004, FR-005, FR-010, FR-014, FR-015, NFR-001, NFR-009 |
